# discovery — prepare questions that test hypotheses, not a script

Prepare a discovery or qualification call around what we need to learn: is this a fit, why now, who decides, what it is worth. The call should also feed the home.

## Use when

- "Prep me for a discovery call", "what should I ask this prospect?", "qualification questions", "first meeting with X".

## Reads

`ICP.md` (fit, committee, disqualifiers), `why-now`, `POSITIONING.md` (objections), `accounts/<company>.md` if present, any notes the person supplies. Use web research on the account when tools allow, with sources.

## Steps

1. **State what we think we know** about the account as hypotheses, each with its evidence level: fit, trigger, pain, committee, alternative they'd use.
2. **Turn each hypothesis into one or two open questions** that could disprove it. Prefer "walk me through the last time…" over "do you have a problem with…".
3. **Order the call:** their situation → what changed (why now) → the cost of the problem → what they've tried → who else is involved and how they decide → what a good outcome looks like → agreed next step.
4. **Prepare the listening list** — words and signals that would confirm or kill the opportunity. Include the disqualifiers.
5. **Prepare two short proof stories** from `PROOF.md` that fit the likely pain, within their permission.
6. **Prepare the capture template** for after the call: verbatim pains, trigger, committee, alternatives, next step, and which `ICP.md` hypotheses moved.

## Output

A one-page call plan: hypotheses, questions in order, listening list, two proof stories, capture template.

## Don't

- Don't pitch in discovery. One short answer when asked, then back to their situation.
- Don't present web research as known fact on the call; frame it as "I read that…, is that right?"

## Could compound

After the call: the capture goes to `field/`, and confirmed or broken hypotheses become `Heard` lines via `compound`.
