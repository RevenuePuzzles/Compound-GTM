# inbox — does it survive a crowded inbox?

The two-second test. The reader sees a sender name, a subject line and a preview, and decides to open, archive or report. Then, if opened, about five seconds to decide whether to keep reading.

## Use when

- Any outbound email, LinkedIn message, nurture email or subject-line set before it goes out.
- "Will this get opened?", "does this sound like AI?", "is this spammy?"

## Checks

### The two seconds

- **Sender + subject + preview** read as a person writing to a person? Or as a campaign?
- Does the preview text add information, or repeat the subject?
- Would the subject be embarrassing if the recipient forwarded it to their boss?

### The five seconds

- Is the first line about the reader, or about the sender?
- Can the reader tell what this is and what's being asked without scrolling on a phone?
- Is there one ask, or several?

### Template and machine tells

Flag phrases that mark mass outreach or machine writing, for example: "I hope this finds you well", "I came across your profile", "I wanted to reach out", "quick question" with no question, "just following up", "circling back", "in today's fast-paced landscape", "unlock", "supercharge", "game-changer", "seamless", "leverage", "delve", em-dash-heavy rhythm, triplets of adjectives, rhetorical questions that answer themselves, and fake familiarity ("loved your recent post") not backed by real research.

### Deliverability risks

Many links or images, link shorteners, attachments in a first email, ALL CAPS, heavy punctuation, spam-trigger words around money and urgency, tracking pixels on very cold sends, missing plain-text feel. Note these as risks to check with the sending setup — this lens cannot see domain reputation or authentication.

### The list

- How big is the list against the whole reachable market? A first email to a small, senior market is a one-time chance per person — say so when the draft isn't ready.
- Merge fields have fallbacks (no "Hi ,").
- Send volume and pacing per sending domain are sensible; a large identical batch from one domain is a risk.

### Follow-ups

Does each follow-up add something new (an insight, a proof, a different angle), or just bump?

## Output

```
Two-second verdict: open / archive / report — why
Five-second verdict: keep reading / stop — at which line
Tells found: [line → why it reads as template or machine → plain alternative]
Deliverability flags: …
The one fix that matters most: …
```
