# sequence — write outbound and nurture sequences that sound like a person

Draft email, LinkedIn and call sequences grounded in the home: buyer words from `signals`, triggers from `why-now`, proof from `PROOF.md`, tone from `VOICE.md`.

## Use when

- "Write a cold email", "outbound sequence", "follow-ups", "LinkedIn messages", "nurture emails", "re-engage closed-lost".

## Reads

The motion's `BRIEF.md` (if any), `ICP.md`, `POSITIONING.md`, `PROOF.md`, `VOICE.md`, `PLAYS.md` (openers and angles that worked or failed before).

## Steps

For a single email, skip to step 3 and keep the rest light — one audience line, one trigger, the email, and the two most likely reply branches.

1. **Fix the audience and trigger.** One role, one situation. If the person asks for a sequence "for everyone", ask which role first, or write for the best-fit role and say so.
2. **Choose one angle per touch.** Each touch has one job: earn attention (trigger + pain), give something useful (insight, benchmark from `PROOF.md`, a resource), prove (case, result), or ask clearly. Never all four at once.
3. **Write each touch:**
   - Subject lines that read like a colleague wrote them: short, lowercase, about their situation; no clickbait, no emoji, no brand name.
   - Open with them, not us: the trigger or the pain in their words.
   - One claim, backed. Tag it in a comment with its `PROOF.md` entry.
   - One ask, easy to answer.
   - Short. A first email that needs scrolling on a phone is too long.
4. **Personalisation slots** — mark what must be researched per contact (`{{trigger detail}}`) versus what can be fixed per segment. Never fake personalisation ("loved your recent post") that wasn't researched.
5. **Write the branches** — positive reply, "not now", "not me", objection, unsubscribe. The reply handling is where meetings are won.
6. **Self-check** with `inbox` and `buyer` lenses before handing over. Fix what they find.

## Output

The sequence (day · channel · subject · body · goal), personalisation slots, reply branches, and the claims used with their proof entries.

## Don't

- Don't use `Believed` claims as fact, invented stats, or fake familiarity.
- Don't write anything the team couldn't send under their own name.

## Could compound

After results: openers and angles that worked into `PLAYS.md`; phrases buyers repeated back into `VOICE.md`.
