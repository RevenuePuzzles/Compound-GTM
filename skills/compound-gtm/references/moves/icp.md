# icp — define who buys, who doesn't, and why

Build or sharpen the ideal customer profile from evidence — closed deals first, opinions last — and make it usable for targeting, qualification and messaging.

## Use when

- "Who should we target?", "our ICP is too broad", "pipeline is full of the wrong companies", "build personas".
- Fit-checking a specific account or deal against the profile.

## Reads

`ICP.md`, `COMPANY.md`, CRM exports of won and lost deals, `field/` notes, `PLAYS.md` results by segment.

## Steps

1. **Start from outcomes, not wishes.** If deal data exists, segment closed-won and closed-lost by size, industry, region, source, use case. Compare win rate, deal size, cycle length and retention where available. Report counts.
2. **Find the clone list.** Identify the five to ten best customers (fast to close, kept paying, expanded, referred). What do they share that losers and churners don't?
3. **Write the profile in four layers:**
   - **Company fit** — the few firmographic and technographic traits that actually predicted success. Fewer is better; three sharp traits beat twelve soft ones.
   - **Situation** — the state the company is in when it needs us (just merged, new CFO, re-tendering a vendor, losing a client). This is what makes targeting work.
   - **Buying committee** — roles involved, what each cares about in their own words, who signs, who can block. Mark unknown roles as unknown.
   - **Disqualifiers** — hard noes, with the reason and evidence. Most teams skip this; it saves the most time.
4. **Grade every line** with its evidence level. Flag the `Believed` lines that matter most and how to test each.
5. **Make it operational.** Translate the profile into: a list-building query (fields a data tool can filter on), three qualification questions for a first call, and a one-line "this is a fit when…" test.
6. **Fit check (when an account is given):** score the account against each layer as fit / partial / no / unknown, with reasons. Do not produce a single magic number.

## Output

Updated `ICP.md` content (or a proposed diff), the operational translation, and the top open hypotheses with a cheap test for each.

## Don't

- Don't define the ICP by who you'd like to sell to. Aspirational segments go under "Expansion hypotheses", tagged `Believed`.
- Don't create persona fiction (names, hobbies, coffee orders). Stick to situation, pains, priorities and influence.

## Could compound

New disqualifiers; segment-level win-rate observations; pains confirmed in buyer words.
