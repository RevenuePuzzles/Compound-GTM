# rival — read it as your competitor's best salesperson

Your strongest competitor's top rep has just been forwarded your email, page or proposal by the prospect. How do they take you apart in the next call?

## Use when

- Positioning, battlecards, proposals in competitive deals, comparison pages, launch messaging.
- "How would X respond to this?", "find our weak spots".

## Set up the rival

Pick the main alternative from `POSITIONING.md` or `alternatives` — or the one the person names. Remember the most dangerous rival is often "do nothing" or "build it yourselves"; offer that lens when no vendor is named. Use only verified facts about the rival (from the home or live web checks, dated). If capabilities are unverified, say so rather than assuming.

## The read

As the rival's rep, write:

1. **The reframe** — how they recast the problem so their strength matters most and ours least.
2. **The landmines** — two or three questions they'd coach the prospect to ask us, aimed at our weakest claims.
3. **The FUD** — the fear they'd plant (risk, size, lock-in, maturity, cost of switching) and the line in our material that makes it believable.
4. **The concession** — what they'd admit we do well, to look fair, before pivoting.
5. **The close** — why they'd tell the prospect to choose them anyway.

Then step out of character:

- Which attacks land because of something in our own material?
- Which are fair, and need a real answer (proof, offer change), not better copy?
- The pre-emptive move: the line or proof to add so the attack loses its force.

## Output

The rival's playbook against us (short), the honest assessment, and three pre-emptive changes with their proof sources.

## Principles

- Make the rival smart. A strawman teaches nothing.
- Don't publish anything from this lens. It's for preparation.

## Could compound

Landmine questions and answers into `POSITIONING.md`; verified rival facts with dates; new alternatives discovered.
